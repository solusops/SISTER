\begin{abstract}
Large language models perform worse when instructions arrive gradually across a conversation instead of all at once, but this ``getting lost in conversation'' effect has mostly been studied on tasks with a single verifiable answer. We ask whether it also holds for open-ended creative writing, where no such ground truth exists. We build a benchmark of 160 creative-writing tasks across six genres, each with a fully-specified instruction and a matched sharded version that delivers the same content across five to nine conversational turns. Six open-weight models generate under both conditions, yielding 1{,}920 outputs and 960 matched pairs, scored by a blinded judge against each task's atomic constraints and five writing-quality dimensions. Full delivery substantially improves constraint adherence and produces higher craft, structure/coherence, and genre effectiveness, while sharded delivery scores slightly higher on originality. Restricting the comparison to pairs with equal observed adherence removes the craft advantage entirely and reverses the originality, genre-effectiveness, and characterization gaps, but a structure/coherence gap persists---evidence that constraint forgetting alone does not explain the degradation, and that progressive specification carries an additional cost to narrative organization. Because these creative-quality conclusions rest on automated judgment, we validate it against a stratified sample of human annotations and two independently-designed pairwise protocols: judgments track human preference considerably better for constraint following (63.3\% directional agreement, $\kappa=0.544$) than for holistic creative quality (40.0\%, $\kappa=0.078$), and order-swap audits show the same asymmetry at the level of individual judgments. Multi-turn creative writing therefore degrades along at least two distinct axes, and reliably measuring the second remains an open problem.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} large language models, multi-turn conversation, creative writing evaluation, instruction following, LLM-as-a-judge
